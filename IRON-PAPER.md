# The Iron Paper

*On eironeia, cleverness, and what is left when the joke stops.*

A paper about feigned ignorance, which feigns nothing.

Lead is soft, and it is what you use when you want a thing to be heavy rather
than strong. This one is iron. Every claim below names its source, every
distinction is a question you can answer yourself, and the machine that sorts
them is in the same folder and will contradict this text if this text is wrong.

There is an obvious joke available here, that a document about irony ought to
mean the opposite of what it says. It declines. The joke is the point: the only
honest paper on this subject is one that says exactly what it means, because
the subject is what happens when people do not.

---

## 1. The word began as an accusation

Eironeia is not a compliment in Greek. An eiron is a dissembler, the one who understates what he is. His opposite is the alazon, the braggart who overstates, and Aristotle puts truthfulness between them as the mean (Nicomachean Ethics 1108a, and Book IV.7).

When Thrasymachus throws the word at Socrates in Republic 337a he is not admiring a technique. He is saying: stop dodging. Every later sense of the word, including the flattering ones, is built on top of that insult.

## 3. The distinction that does the work

A lie and verbal irony have the same shape: said one thing, meant another. They differ by one answer only. The lie needs the gap to go unnoticed. The irony needs it noticed. Intent to be caught is the whole boundary between deceiving someone and telling them something.

This is the only place in the table where two rows share a shape and part on a single answer, and it is not a technicality. It is the whole ethical content of the subject. Everything else here is taxonomy.

## 4. Cleverness, money, and wisdom

*Cleverness is universally convertible, and that is exactly why it cannot tell you where to go.*

### 4.1 Aristotle already separated them

Nicomachean Ethics 1144a. Deinotes, cleverness, is the faculty of hitting whatever mark you set. Aristotle is explicit that it is neutral: if the mark is noble the cleverness is praiseworthy, and if the mark is base the same faculty is mere unscrupulousness, panourgia. Phronesis, practical wisdom, is not a different amount of cleverness. It is cleverness plus a mark worth hitting.

> Cleverness is the aim. Wisdom is the aiming at something.

### 4.2 Which is why the money comparison is exact

Money is the universal equivalent. It converts into anything, and for that reason it says nothing about what to want. Its power is its emptiness. Cleverness is the same instrument in another medium: convertible into any end, silent on which end. Ask how far universal cleverness gets you and the answer is precise. All the way to any destination, and no distance at all toward knowing which destination.

> A universal medium buys everything and recommends nothing.

### 4.3 The Socratic answer to what wisdom is

Apology 21d. Socrates is told he is the wisest, and concludes he is wiser only in this: he does not think he knows what he does not know. Human wisdom is not a stock of answers. It is an accurate register of where the answers stop.

> Wisdom is knowing the edge of what you know, and saying so.

### 4.4 And this is where the two halves meet

That claim is why the oldest fight about Socrates never ends. If he truly does not know, the profession of ignorance is honest. If he does know, it is eironeia in the old pejorative sense, a costume. Vlastos called the live version complex irony: he both means it and does not. The engine here refuses to settle that. It marks it U.

> The unresolved case is the case, not a gap in the table.

### 4.5 What a third face is for

A system with only true and false must answer every question, so it answers some of them falsely. A third face, unknown, is the formal version of the Socratic move: a place to put what you have not established, so that it does not get silently promoted into a yes. Cleverness fills the gaps. Wisdom marks them.

> U is not a missing answer. It is an answer about you.

So the ranking is not cleverness then wisdom, as though wisdom were more cleverness. Cleverness answers how. Wisdom answers whether. You can have an unlimited amount of the first and none of the second, and the age is full of people proving it.

## 5. Today

Irony stopped being a device and became weather.

Booth in 1974 could still describe a stable kind, where you flip the sentence
and find the meaning waiting underneath, and an unstable kind where nothing is
waiting. Hutcheon in 1994 described the next stage, in which the quotation marks
never come off, and there is no original being quoted.

The cost is specific and it is not aesthetic. Stable irony is a way of telling
someone something. Saturated irony is a way of never being answerable for
anything, because nothing was ever asserted. That is why Wallace in 1993 called
it a cage rather than a liberation, and why the move that reads as radical now
is meaning it.

Which is the situation the last row of the table describes. Sincerity, in a room
where everyone is protected, is the only remaining way to be caught.

## 6. What this paper does not claim

This paper does not claim that the table is complete. It claims
that each row in it is checkable.

It does not settle whether Socrates was honest. It marks that unknown, on
purpose, and the engine will keep marking it unknown until someone supplies the
answer rather than the confidence.

It does not claim that irony is bad. Two of the rows are how literature works.

It claims one thing only, and it is the line the engine enforces: the difference
between deceiving a person and telling them something is whether you meant the
gap to be found.

## 7. One that happened while this was written

The test suite for this repository includes one rule: the page
must make no network call. The first version of that rule searched the whole
file for the string fetch( and failed immediately.

It failed because the page's own footer invites the reader to search it for
fetch( and find nothing. The sentence denying the thing contained the thing.
The checker was reading a document and reporting on code, and those are not the
same object.

This is worth recording because it is the subject of this paper happening to the
paper. A claim was made in good faith, in plain language, with no intent to
mislead, and a literal reader still got the wrong answer, because the mention of
a word and the use of a word are different acts. Verbal irony depends on that
difference and so does every quotation, every joke, and every denial.

The fix was not to delete the sentence. The fix was to make the checker look
inside the script and the attributes, where a network call would have to live,
and leave the prose alone. A checker that cannot tell mention from use will
either miss the real thing or convict the honest one, and it does both for the
same reason.
